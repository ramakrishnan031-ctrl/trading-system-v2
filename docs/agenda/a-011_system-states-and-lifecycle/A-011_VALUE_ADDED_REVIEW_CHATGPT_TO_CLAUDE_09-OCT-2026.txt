3. A-011 LEGACY MANUAL — REQUIRED WORDING / AUDIT CLARIFICATIONS
============================================================

3.1 Section 3 / Section 8.1 — HARD_KILL semantics
Current summary says HARD_KILL “blocks all orders and dispatches the flatten worker.” This can be read as contradictory because the flatten worker must submit exit orders.

Suggested clarification:
“Under HARD_KILL, ordinary order/entry processing is blocked and the asynchronous emergency-flatten worker is dispatched. The worker has its own emergency exit path. This does not establish that every flatten order succeeds; broker access, rejection, missing price data and the recorded market-protection defect can prevent or delay flattening.”

Use more exact wording if the inspected call path supports it. Do not imply HARD_KILL guarantees a flat book.

3.2 Section 8.2 and final statement — CNC / broker protection qualification
The draft correctly identifies the same-symbol mixed-book defect: the flatten can act on broker net quantity by symbol and sell part of CNC quantity even though CNC is normally spared.

The final statement currently says: “A stop, a crash or a HALT ended position management in the process; positions then rested on orders at the broker.”
This is too broad if read as a guarantee that all positions were protected. A process stop ends in-process management, but whether broker-side protective orders existed, remained valid, matched actual quantity, or were sufficient is position-specific and may be UNKNOWN. The manual records the 03-Sep naked-position incident and a market-protection failure.

Suggested replacement:
“A stop, crash or HALT ended in-process management. Any protection then depended on the broker-side orders that actually remained active; the source does not establish that every residual position was protected, and the 03-Sep incident demonstrates a path where protection was lost and the emergency exit was rejected.”

3.3 Section 6 — startup clears and HALT
The distinction is sound:
- every prior-calendar-day kill is cleared at startup, including HARD_KILL;
- same-day scheduled SOFT_KILL can be cleared only under the stated reason/flat-book condition;
- same-day HARD_KILL and other same-day emergency kills can leave the system in HALT;
- a prior-day exit 4 can be attempted again by the watcher, and startup clearing can then allow the next start.

Retain the source-reported 20-Jun decision and do not present Rama’s Q-23 recollection as the actual source rule. This is legacy fact only; the V2 policy remains open.

3.4 Section 7 — systemd restart-rate interpretation
The table is useful. The statement that exit 1/2/6/9 “would restart about every 10 seconds all day” is an interpretation of the repository unit plus systemd defaults, not a verified installed-VM runtime result. The draft labels it as an audit lead and notes installed overrides are unknown. Keep that evidence boundary prominent; do not promote this to an observed incident without a record.

3.5 Section 8.3 — kill trigger inventory
This is a valuable improvement. Preserve the separation between:
- fund-manager drift thresholds that escalate to SOFT_KILL/HARD_KILL;
- reconciler G3 capital-drift alerts that do not escalate;
- inert live-feed direct kill calls in the inspected construction;
- cross-process database writes that do not alter the running process’s memory-authoritative kill state.

Do not shorten this inventory in a way that reintroduces the old generalisation that capital drift only alerted or that every caller was effective.

3.6 Section 10 / A-003 erratum E-1 — service self-exit
The post-25-Aug predicate is described with the necessary identity qualifications: cleanly identified CNC carry does not keep the service alive; unresolved/conflicting identities and fallback behavior can. The pre-25-Aug defect is supported by dated records.

Keep the exact temporal boundary. Do not say the delivery-carry defect persisted at the baseline. Do not infer how often it happened; logs do not survive.

3.7 Section 12 — webhook gate and signal outcomes
Retain three separate outcomes:
- webhook-door refusal: no signal row; webhook audit/request counters only;
- clean stop / kill / expiry: a signal can receive a terminal rejection status;
- crash: in-memory queue lost while database rows can remain non-terminal.

Do not summarise all three as “signals were dropped.” Do not imply every refused request has a per-signal record.

3.8 Section 13 — authentication failure
The source supports the different responses of token monitor, order monitor and reconciler. The “token rename” flag having no identified caller is correctly labelled as a cross-topic defect/erratum rather than a functioning control.

Do not say valid authentication is unnecessary for position management. The accurate legacy statement is that broker calls require valid authentication; already-resting broker orders may continue to exist, but local monitoring/reconciliation or replacement orders cannot be assumed to work without broker access.

3.9 Section 14 — startup reconciliation and duplicate prevention
The description of broker-tag recovery, capital release/adoption and no signal requeue is appropriately source-specific. “Whether a restart ever produced a duplicate: UNKNOWN” must remain, even though safeguards exist.

For uncertain in-flight entries, do not imply successful flattening; describe only that the code path invokes the emergency handling documented elsewhere.

3.10 Section 15 — status sets
Keep statuses grouped by entity (signal, trade, order), as the draft does. Do not present the signals list as a strict exhaustive enum because the manual says the set is open by design and includes status families/prefixes.

3.11 Section 17 — uncertain exits

The sequence—protection cancelled, no exit submitted in the first pass, recovery SL, second cancellation, emergency MARKET rejections for missing market protection, human close—supports the conclusion that the process remained active but did not guarantee safe completion.

The statement that the scheduled EOD fire does not repeat the same day after partial failure should remain scoped to the inspected path, with restart-recovery/fire_now exceptions retained.

“Exit 5 is not restarted” is true of the repository systemd unit, but not of the token watcher, which retries exit 5 up to three times per rolling hour and then warns once per day. Do not describe this as observed production frequency; it is the source-defined restart policy.

A-010 E-1 — ACCEPT.
