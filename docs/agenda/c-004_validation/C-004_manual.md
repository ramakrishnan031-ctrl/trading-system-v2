## 4. Where validation sits on the intake path (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED)
_handle_webhook reads the body first; the 1 MiB limit (R 251) is enforced at that read (R 480), and the shutdown 503 and the per-IP 429 answer before _process_request runs (their audit rows are written at R 487-489 and 497-499). _process_request then runs, in order: authentication (R 547-549, 401), the unknown-scanner check (R 551-554, 404), the EOD branch (R 562-564; C-011), the kill switch (R 566-568, 403), backpressure (R 570-578, 503) and the entry window (R 580-583, 403); then the request-level checks of section 5 (R 585-674) and the per-symbol checks of section 6: the loop (R 696-728) and the start of _process_signal (R 904-926). All intake validation is in R. C-003 section 7.3 (frozen) is the full table of the gates' answers and is not repeated here; this manual adds the field-level and symbol-level detail.

## 5. Request-level checks (the whole POST; M-1)

### 5.1 The checks (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED unless marked; trace rows T-01 to T-09, section 16.2)
They run in this order inside _process_request, after the gates of section 4:

| # | Check | Lines | Fails with | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | JSON parse | R 585-593 | 400 "Malformed JSON: <parser message>" | the WARNING at 589-592 logs the first 500 raw bytes; Python's parser accepts NaN literals and keeps the last of duplicate keys (CURRENT EXTERNAL FACT, probe 1) |
| 2 | Object | R 595-596 | 400 "Request body must be a JSON object" | a top-level array parses and fails here |
| 3 | FIX-074 critical cast | R 598-606; 419-444 | 400 "Invalid payload: critical field <name>=<value> cannot be cast to float: <reason>"; an OverflowError (a JSON number too large for a float) is not caught (443) and ends in 500 | only top-level price and entry_price, when present and not null |
| 4 | FIX-074 other casts | R 446-469 | a ValueError or TypeError sets the value to None with a WARNING; an OverflowError (a huge JSON number; Infinity in qty) is not caught (451, 464) and ends in 500 | trigger_price, sl_pct, target_pct (float), qty (int); nothing later reads them (T-04); OverflowError: CURRENT EXTERNAL FACT, probe 2 |
| 5 | Required keys | R 608-615 | 400 "Missing required field: '<name>'" | stocks, trigger_prices, triggered_at; presence only (a null passes here) |
| 6 | scan_name | R 617-633; 111-138 | 400 "scan_name in body does not match scanner_name path param"; a non-string value: AttributeError at 622 -> 500 (525-528) | optional; compared after lower(), spaces to underscores and the strip of a leading "<account tag>-" or "<account tag>_" (WR4b) |
| 7 | triggered_at | R 635-662 | 400 "Invalid triggered_at; expected HH:MM am/pm or YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS" | str() of the value, stripped; formats "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S", "%I:%M %p", "%H:%M"; a time-only value takes today's date; a naive value is set to IST |
| 8 | Types | R 664-668 | 400 "stocks and trigger_prices must be comma-separated strings" | a JSON list or number fails |
| 9 | Split and length | R 670-674 | 400 "stocks and trigger_prices list length mismatch" | split on ",", strip each; empty elements are kept and reach the per-symbol checks |

- The FIX-074 casts (rows 3 and 4) work only on the top-level body. The realistic Chartink body of the tests (tests/unit/test_webhook_receiver.py 1730-1742) carries none of their fields, so on such a body they do nothing, and they are not applied to the comma-split prices of row 9 (T-03). The comment at R 599 says the cast "Handles both top-level numeric fields and per-signal fields if present"; the code works only on the dict it is given (R 419-471, 600) (K-03).
- An overflowing cast (rows 3 and 4) ends in 500, not 400, because the casts catch only ValueError and TypeError (CURRENT EXTERNAL FACT, probe 2, for Python's OverflowError). An edge case (L-14): C-001 line 210, C-002 section 9 row 11 and C-003 section 7.3 row 8 list the cast among the 400s, which holds for price and entry_price failing with ValueError or TypeError (section 24).
- A JSON null passes the presence check of row 5 and fails later: a null triggered_at at row 7 (str(None) is "None", which matches no format; T-07), a null stocks or trigger_prices at row 8 (T-05).

### 5.2 What a request-level failure leaves (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED; also C-002 section 10.5 and C-003 sections 7.3 and 7.5, frozen)
No signals row and no queue entry. For every 400, _handle_webhook logs "webhook/<name>: 400 response | raw_body=<first 1,000 bytes>" at WARNING (R 518-523); check 1 logs its own WARNING with the first 500 raw bytes (R 589-592; C-003 section 7.3 row 7, "two WARNINGs"), and checks 3, 5, 6 and 7 a WARNING of their own (keys or values, no raw body). The audit row records the response code with accepted 0 and rejected 0 (R 510-534). A 500 (a non-string scan_name, an overflowing cast) logs one CRITICAL line instead (R 526) and no raw-body WARNING, and is audited as (500, 0, 0).

### 5.3 Fields the receiver does not check (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED; a grep of R for the field names)
scan_url and alert_name are never read; webhook_url is touched only by the storage sanitiser, which blanks it in the stored copy (R 681-690 and the sanitiser's lines 860, 869, 879 and 882, seen in that grep; C-003 section 11.4). Extra keys are ignored. C-001 section 4.3 and C-002 section 8.2 (frozen) already record that the receiver reads only stocks, trigger_prices, triggered_at and the optional scan_name, whatever the Content-Type. Nothing checks that the body's symbols belong to the scanner, that triggered_at is not in the future (C-009), or that a symbol repeats within one payload (C-005 handles repeats as IN_PROCESS or DUPLICATE).

## 6. Per-symbol checks and their outcomes (M-2)

### 6.1 Order (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED; trace rows T-10 to T-15)
For each pair zipped from the two lists (R 696):
1. alias (R 701-706; section 7);
2. exclusion (R 708-717; section 8) -> REJECTED_EXCLUDED_SYMBOL;
3. empty symbol (R 904-906) -> INVALID_SYMBOL;
4. price (R 908-914): float(); a ValueError or TypeError, or a value <= 0 -> INVALID_PRICE;
5. expiry (R 916-926) -> EXPIRED (C-009 owns the rule);
6. the in-flight claim (R 934-935) -> IN_PROCESS; the cache dedup (R 940-947) -> DUPLICATE; the INSERT (R 963-1044) -> DUPLICATE, STORE_ERROR, or the re-queue of an earlier QUEUE_FULL row (ACCEPTED or QUEUE_FULL); the queue (R 1047-1068) -> QUEUE_FULL; otherwise ACCEPTED (R 1072). C-005 to C-007 own these steps.

Steps 1 and 2 run in the loop; steps 3 to 6 in _process_signal. The resolved symbol (B2) is used from step 1 on, in the response, the row and the queue.

### 6.2 The only symbol check is emptiness (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED)
`if not symbol` (R 905). An empty element comes from an empty stocks string, a doubled comma or a trailing comma (CURRENT EXTERNAL FACT, probe 1: "".split(",") is [""]; "A,B," ends with ""). A symbol of spaces only is stripped to empty first (R 670). FACT (TEST SOURCE): the test with " ,TCS" asserts INVALID_SYMBOL, then ACCEPTED (test_empty_symbol_returns_invalid_symbol, tests/unit/test_webhook_receiver.py 992-1005). Nothing at intake (signals/webhook_receiver.py) checks characters, length, case, a suffix or presence in the instrument master (N-1, N-2); the later stops are in section 10.

### 6.3 Price (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED; the parser's behaviour CURRENT EXTERNAL FACT, probes 1 and 2)
The per-symbol price is the matching element of trigger_prices (a string), not the FIX-074 fields. float() accepts surrounding spaces, exponents ("1e3"), underscores ("1_000"), non-ASCII digits, and "nan", "inf" and "Infinity"; of these only "-inf" fails "<= 0". So a NaN or +inf trigger price passes intake and is queued as the float (R 1047). Stored, it is bound into the REAL column trigger_price (R 974-977), where Python's sqlite3 with SQLite 3.45.1 stores a NaN as NULL and +inf as an infinity (probe 2); the VMs' SQLite version is not established (L-01). What a NaN or +inf price did after the queue was not traced (U-07). FACT (TEST SOURCE): zero (953), negative (966) and non-numeric (979) prices are INVALID_PRICE, and in a mixed batch BADSTOCK at -1.0 is INVALID_PRICE between two accepted symbols (198-216), all in tests/unit/test_webhook_receiver.py.

### 6.5 Hand-off (FACT, SOURCE-VERIFIED)
An ACCEPTED symbol leaves one signals row and one queue tuple. The row is a new one (signal_id, symbol = B3, scanner and strategy both set to the path's scanner name, triggered_at, received_at, expires_at, status QUEUED, fingerprint, fingerprint_date, trigger_price = the parsed float (a NaN stored as NULL, 6.3), webhook_payload = the sanitised raw body; R 963-980), or on the re-queue path the existing row (6.4). The tuple is (signal_id, scanner_name, symbol, price, triggered_at) (R 1047; R 993 on the re-queue path), which P unpacks in _process_one (P 867-887). The next consumer is SignalProcessor (section 10.1; C-008).

### 16.3 End-to-end walkthrough: one Chartink POST on the pinned configuration (the code path, not an observed request)
What any one request on any one day did is not observed; this is the code path for a POST from a configured scanner that passes the gates of section 4 (inside the entry window, the kill switch off, the queue below its backpressure limit).
1. The body has the Chartink shape of the tests (tests/unit/test_webhook_receiver.py 1730-1742): stocks and trigger_prices as comma-separated strings, triggered_at, scan_name, scan_url, alert_name and webhook_url (B1; C-002 sections 4 and 8).
2. _handle_webhook reads the body (413 above 1 MiB, with no audit row), then the shutdown 503 and the per-IP 429 (section 4).
3. _process_request: authentication, the unknown-scanner 404, the EOD branch, the kill-switch 403, backpressure 503 and the window 403 (section 4; C-003 section 7.3).
4. The request-level checks T-01 to T-09, in order (5.1). Any failure answers 400, or 500 for a non-string scan_name or an overflowing cast, with no signals row and no queue entry, and the audit row (code, 0, 0) (5.2).
5. Each zipped pair (6.1): the element is stripped; an element "tvsscs" or "TVSSCS" becomes TVSSRICHAK (K-06), while "reliance" stays "reliance" (7.3, 7.4; probe 1); an element "gvpil" is REJECTED_EXCLUDED_SYMBOL with a DEBUG line (8.2); an empty element from a doubled or trailing comma is INVALID_SYMBOL (6.2); a trigger price "0" or "-5" is INVALID_PRICE, while "nan" and "inf" pass (6.3); then expiry, the claims, dedup, the INSERT and the queue (6.1, 6.5).
6. The batch answer: 200, or 503 if any symbol was QUEUE_FULL or STORE_ERROR (section 11); the audit row with the body's counts for a 200, or (503, 0, 0) (section 12).
7. SignalProcessor takes each tuple (10.1); for an MIS-intent signal whose symbol is on the learned blocklist, the screener's MIS pre-drop logs a would-drop, and on the pinned configuration the signal continues (10.3); the quote fetch builds "NSE:<symbol>": a symbol with no quote stops as SKIPPED_QUOTE_UNAVAILABLE, before sizing and order placement (10.2). A TVSSRICHAK that came from TVSSCS would be requested as "NSE:TVSSRICHAK"; whether the broker would return a quote for it is UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED (U-03): with no quote it would stop here as SKIPPED_QUOTE_UNAVAILABLE, and with one it would go on to the later gates, outside C-004 (10.4). Whether such a signal ever arrived is not established (U-09).
8. Sizing and order placement look the symbol up in the cache and fall back on a miss (9.5); G-002 and the later topics own them.

## 27. Final C-004 statement (a summary of sections 4 to 21; each claim's label as stated there)
> At the baseline, all intake validation of a Chartink POST is in signals/webhook_receiver.py, after the gates that C-003 records. The whole request fails, before any symbol is handled, when the body is not JSON or not a JSON object, when a top-level price or entry_price cannot be cast to float, when stocks, trigger_prices or triggered_at is missing as a key, when an optional scan_name does not match the path after normalising and the account-prefix strip, when triggered_at matches none of three formats, when stocks or trigger_prices is not a string, or when the two comma-split lists differ in length: each answers 400, except a non-string scan_name and an overflowing cast, which answer 500, and none leaves a signals row or a queue entry. Each symbol is then stripped, replaced by its upper-cased alias target when its upper-cased form is one of the two keys of config/symbol_aliases.yaml, and rejected as REJECTED_EXCLUDED_SYMBOL when it is one of five excluded symbols in any case, as INVALID_SYMBOL when it is empty, as INVALID_PRICE when its trigger price does not parse as a float or is zero or less, and as EXPIRED when it is too old; these rejections leave no row, no queue entry and no log line, except one DEBUG line for an exclusion, and reach the audit only inside a 200's rejected count. Nothing at intake checks a symbol's characters, length, case, suffix or exchange, or looks it up in the instrument master, and a "nan" or "inf" trigger price passes. A symbol that passes is stored and queued exactly as resolved; the batch answers 200, or 503 when any symbol is QUEUE_FULL or STORE_ERROR, and a 503 is audited as (503, 0, 0) even when other symbols in it were accepted. The instrument master, built by scripts/refresh_instruments.py (declared at 09:00 on weekdays) and loaded once at start-up with no reload in the process, is looked up later by exact, case-sensitive key, and every consumer read falls back on a miss (lot size 1, tick 0.05, token 0, sector "UNKNOWN") instead of rejecting the symbol. The first later stop for a symbol the broker does not return is the secondary screener's quote fetch, which marks it SKIPPED_QUOTE_UNAVAILABLE before sizing and order placement; MIS eligibility is learned from the broker's rejection at placement and applied at the screener, in shadow mode on the pinned configuration.
