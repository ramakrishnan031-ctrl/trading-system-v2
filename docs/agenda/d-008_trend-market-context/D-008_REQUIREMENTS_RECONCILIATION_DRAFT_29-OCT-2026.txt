2. HOW Q1 FITS WITH Q4, Q5 AND Q9
========================================================================================================================
What Rama said (RAMA REQUIREMENT INPUT):
- Q1: "No market-context input in trade decisions".
- Q4: proceed without the context and alert at once on Telegram and email; include the condition in the pre-flight
  checks.
- Q5: record context with every signal and trade.
- Q9: the briefing is information only.

How they fit (INTERPRETATION):
- Q1 is read as keeping market context out of Trade / Reject qualification. Q4, Q5 and Q9 are about observing
  context: recording it, reporting its absence and showing it in a briefing. The four answers are compatible if
  market context is observability and recording only and never changes Trade / Reject qualification.
- Proposed clarification, not settled: the exclusion in Q1 might also cover sizing, ranking / priority and other
  trade attributes. Rama's answer does not say so, so this is OPEN (OQ8-12). Until he answers, neither the wider
  exclusion nor its opposite is recorded as his requirement.

What follows for the requirements record:
- Missing, late or suspect market-context data (Q4). That context is not used. The otherwise-qualified process
  continues without a market-context-derived decision change. The data-quality condition is recorded, and the
  requested alerts are issued. What this means for sizing, ranking / priority and other attributes is OPEN
  (OQ8-12).
- The pre-flight check (Q4). The market-context check is warning / alert-only. It cannot block a trade or the system
  start solely because market-context data is missing, late or suspect. Otherwise it would act as the hidden gate
  that Q1 rules out (OQ8-04).
  - This applies to the market-context check only. It says nothing about the other pre-flight checks, existing or
    future; they keep whatever authority their own topics give them.
- Rama's rationale for Q1 is recorded as he gave it: a stock that meets its own strategy conditions may rise during
  a broad sell-off. It is not recorded as a claim that the market never affects individual stocks, or that strategy
  qualification guarantees a result.
