TECHNICAL AMBIGUITIES TO RETAIN FOR DESIGN DISCUSSION
These are questions, not corrections or conclusions.

A. "Nearest S&R" needs an exact meaning.
- Nearest by absolute price distance?
- For a long trade, which relevant support below price and resistance above price should be considered? For a short
  trade, which relevant resistance above price and support below price should be considered? This is a question only;
  it approves no selection method.
- Select one timeframe first, or compare candidate levels across timeframes?
- What if a level from one timeframe is closer but another is structurally stronger?
- How are overlapping levels/zones represented?

B. The timeframe ideas are not a validated hierarchy.
- "1Hr + 1D + 1W" (intraday) and "1D + 1W + 1M" (delivery) are Rama's provisional timeframe ideas, not a hierarchy.
- "1Hr > 1D > 1W" could mean priority order, preference, or a set of candidate timeframes. In the ITC example the level
  used was the nearest one in the trade's direction, whatever its timeframe: Rs 250 from 1Hr, then Rs 260 from 1D.
- None of this is to be read as a settled ranking rule. D-007 is BLOCKED / NOT CLOSED, so all timeframe decisions
  remain OPEN / UNRESOLVED.

C. The ITC numerical illustration (Q1) has label and arithmetic inconsistencies.
They are recorded here as inconsistencies in the raw example, not corrected.
- Labels: the example calls the 1Hr level at Rs 250 the "nearest support" while the CMP is Rs 246. For a price below
  Rs 250 that level sits above price, which may make it overhead resistance for a long, depending on the market
  structure.
- Arithmetic at a CMP of Rs 246: the stated R:R is "1:2 (Risk 1: Return 2)", i.e. reward:risk 2:1, with a risk of Rs 5
  and a reward of Rs 10. The target would then be Rs 246 + Rs 10 = Rs 256. The example writes "Rs 246+Rs 5 = Rs 251".
  Both Rs 251 and Rs 256 are above Rs 250, so the stated rejection follows if the rule is that the target must stay
  below that resistance.
- A CMP of Rs 251: with the same Rs 5 risk and 2:1 reward:risk, the target would be Rs 261. That does not fit below the
  stated Rs 260 (1D) level. So the example's statement that the trade can be taken because the 1D level at Rs 260
  "will accommodate R:R" is not consistent with its own figures, and is not to be read as a valid outcome.
- Whether the stop is set by the strategy first (and then checked against S&R) or derived from support is OPEN /
  UNRESOLVED.
- Preserve the example as Rama's rough illustration; do not use it as an approved example or formula.

D. "SL and target within S&R" needs directional definitions.
- For a long trade, a stop is usually below the entry and a target above it. Whether the stop/target must be strictly
  inside, at, or beyond a level or zone requires explicit definition.
- Conflict, preserved: Q7 says "for risk point is above 'support point'", i.e. the SL above support. Q13 gives an SL of
  Rs 99.50 to 99.90 for support at Rs 100, i.e. below support.
- Conflict, preserved: Q2 sets the target at the level itself (a signal at Rs 996, the level at Rs 1,000, "TGT Rs 4").
  Q13 puts the target below resistance (Rs 199.50 to 199.90 for Rs 200).
- Neither interpretation is chosen here; both are for the future design discussion.
- All examples are long-side examples. Short-side symmetry and any exceptions remain OPEN / UNRESOLVED. Do not
  generalise the long-side example to every strategy without discussion.

E. Zone versus point.
Rama's examples often describe a single price, but real S&R may be represented as a range/zone. Whether V2 uses points,
zones, or both belongs in detailed design. No choice is made here.

F. Strategy-specific behavior.
Breakout and pullback strategies can use S&R differently. Need later to define what is common, what is
strategy-specific, and how exceptions are governed and tested.

G. Trade rejection authority.
- Q3 remains not understood and to be discussed later (Rama: "Not understood well, So we will discuss later").
- Q1, Q2, Q7 and Q13 appear to imply a possible concept: reject the trade if its SL/target do not fit relative to S&R.
  This is an interpretation requiring Rama's discussion and explicit decision; it is not a decided role.
- It must not become a live rejection gate by implication. Explicit governance must decide whether S&R is
  informational, affects ranking, affects Trade/Reject, or participates in entry/risk calculations.

H. Data quality and missing levels.
- Rama's preliminary agreement (Q11) covers only recording and alerting the quality condition.
- Whether a signal proceeds, waits, or is rejected when S&R is unavailable, stale, inconsistent, or suspect remains
  OPEN / UNRESOLVED.
- Alerting must not become an implicit trade blocker or system-start blocker. Any pre-flight behavior must be defined so
  it does not accidentally become an unapproved hidden gate.
