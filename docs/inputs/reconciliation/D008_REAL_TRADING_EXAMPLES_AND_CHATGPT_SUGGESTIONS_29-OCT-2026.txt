A SIMPLE DISTINCTION
- Broad market context: what the overall market/index is doing.
- Sector context: what a sector index/group is doing.
- Stock-level trend: what an individual stock is doing.
- Entry/S&R context: whether the stock's entry is late, near the day's high, close to resistance, or has sufficient room to target.
These can interact, but they are not interchangeable. D-008 should not silently take ownership of the other agenda topics.

Q1 — SHOULD BROAD MARKET CONTEXT AFFECT TRADE DECISIONS?

Illustrative scenario:
At 10:15 IST, a stock generates a valid bullish scanner signal. At the same time, NIFTY 50 is falling sharply and many constituents are weak.

Option A — No market-context input in trade decisions:
The system evaluates the stock using the separately approved strategy, rejection, entry, and risk rules. Broad market direction is not an input.
Possible benefit: simpler and less likely to reject a good stock-specific setup just because the index is weak.
Possible cost: the system may take a long trade while broad-market selling is strong.

Option B — Information/recording only:
The signal is evaluated under the other rules, but the system records that the broad index was weak and may show it in reports.
Possible benefit: creates evidence for later analysis without immediately blocking trades.
Possible cost: recording alone does not prevent a trade exposed to broad-market pressure.

Option C — A hard gate:
A separately defined rule could refuse some long entries when the broad market is weak.
Possible benefit: may avoid some trades exposed to broad selling.
Possible cost: can reject strong relative-strength stocks that rise despite a weak index; it can also miss reversals if the market condition is classified badly.

Option D — Adjustment, not hard rejection:
Market context could affect priority or sizing only if a later approved rule defines exactly how.
Possible benefit: avoids an all-or-nothing gate.
Possible cost: adds complexity and can obscure why two otherwise similar signals were treated differently.

CHATGPT SUGGESTION (NOT A DECISION):
For an initial requirements discussion, Option B is the most conservative starting preference: record market context first, but do not let it block or alter trades until there is evidence and an explicit approved rule. If you prefer that market context never enter the system, choose A. If you already require a protective gate, explain that preference under C. Do not select C or D merely because they sound safer; they need measurable rules and validation later.

Q2 — WHICH TRADING PATHS SHOULD IT APPLY TO?

Illustrative scenario:
A stock has an intraday breakout signal. Separately, another signal is intended for a multi-day/positional holding. The broad index may matter differently to the two trades.

Possible answers:
- Intraday only: market context is considered for intraday trades.
- Positional/delivery only: it is considered for those trades.
- Both: it is available to both, with any differences specified later.
- Strategy opt-in: only strategies that explicitly require the information use it.

CHATGPT SUGGESTION:
If you are unsure, say “Not decided / discuss later.” A reasonable requirement to consider is that the data can be recorded consistently, while any decision effect must be explicitly declared by the relevant strategy or later governance. This is not yet an approved architecture or strategy rule.

Q3 — WHICH MARKET REFERENCE MATTERS?

Illustrative scenario:
NIFTY 50 is weak, but a particular sector index is strong and a stock in that sector is also strong. A NIFTY-only view and a sector-aware view may tell different stories.

- NIFTY 50 only: simple broad-market reference.
- Other broad indices too: may represent a wider market picture, but needs a defined universe.
- Sector indices: may reveal whether the stock's group is strong or weak, but this overlaps with sector-strength/concentration questions bookmarked for D-017 / F-007.
- More than one: broader context, but introduces more data-quality and interpretation questions.

CHATGPT SUGGESTION:
If your main concern is broad-market direction, start by stating whether NIFTY 50 alone is sufficient. Do not automatically add sector indices here: sector strength and concentration are separately bookmarked for D-017 / F-007. You can answer “NIFTY 50 first; other indices not decided.”

Q4 — WHAT IF MARKET DATA IS MISSING, LATE, OR SUSPECT?

Illustrative scenario:
The system cannot obtain a fresh NIFTY value, or the last available value is stale. The stock signal itself is available.

- Option A: no new entries that depend on this context. This only makes sense if the later approved design explicitly makes context a required dependency.
- Option B: proceed without the context and alert.
- Option C: record and alert, while leaving the decision to other approved rules.
- Option D: different treatment depending on whether context is informational or a mandatory gate.

CHATGPT SUGGESTION:
Avoid choosing a global “stop all trades” response unless you really intend market data to be mandatory for all relevant trades. A sensible discussion starting point is: missing optional/informational context must be visible in logs and reports; if a later approved rule makes it a required gate, the missing-data behaviour must be specified separately. This is a suggested requirement principle, not a legacy finding.

Q5 — SHOULD CONTEXT BE RECORDED WITH EVERY SIGNAL/TRADE?

Illustrative scenario:
After several weeks, you want to know whether long signals performed differently on days when NIFTY was rising versus falling. If the system did not store the market context associated with each signal, the analysis may not be possible later.

- Yes, whether or not it affects decisions: better for retrospective analysis; requires data definitions, storage, and quality checks.
- Only if used in the decision: less data, but makes comparisons harder when context was not used.
- No: simplest, but forfeits this analysis.

CHATGPT SUGGESTION:
Prefer A if you want evidence to evaluate whether market context is useful before deciding to use it as a gate. Keep in mind that recording is not the same as allowing it to affect a trade.

Q6 — WHAT DO YOU MEAN BY “TREND”?

Illustrative scenario:

- Broad market/index trend: the general market's condition.
- Stock-level trend: the individual stock's direction/structure.
- Both, but separate: keeps the two concepts distinct and lets later topics define ownership.
- Other: describe your intended meaning.
