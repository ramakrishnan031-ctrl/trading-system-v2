TECHNICAL AMBIGUITIES TO RETAIN FOR DESIGN DISCUSSION
These are questions, not corrections or conclusions.

A. “Nearest S&R” needs an exact meaning.
- Nearest by absolute price distance?
- Nearest relevant support for a long and nearest relevant resistance for a long?
- Select one timeframe first, or compare candidate levels across timeframes?
- What if a level from one timeframe is closer but another is structurally stronger?
- How are overlapping levels/zones represented?

B. The proposed timeframe order is not yet a validated hierarchy.
“1Hr > 1D > 1W” could mean priority order, preference, or a set of candidate timeframes. It must not be interpreted as a settled ranking rule. Delivery's 1D + 1W + 1M proposal is also provisional. D-007 is blocked and must remain unresolved.

C. The ITC numerical illustration needs careful reconstruction during design.
The example says CMP ₹246 and a 1-hour S&R point at ₹250, describing it as “nearest support”. For a price below ₹250, that level may instead be overhead resistance depending on the market structure. The example also describes R:R as ₹5 risk / ₹10 reward, implying a ₹251 target from ₹246; whether that arithmetic, direction, stop, target, and level labels are mutually consistent is not established. Preserve it as Rama's rough illustration; do not use it as an approved example or formula.

D. “SL and target within S&R” needs directional definitions.
For a long trade, a stop is usually below the entry and a target above it; support/resistance placement and whether the stop/target must be strictly inside, at, or beyond a zone require explicit definition. Short trades may require the inverse logic. Do not generalise the long-side example to every strategy without discussion.

E. Zone versus point.
Rama's examples often describe a single price, but real S&R may be represented as a range/zone. Whether V2 uses points, zones, or both belongs in detailed design. No choice is made here.

F. Strategy-specific behavior.
Breakout and pullback strategies can use S&R differently. Need later to define what is common, what is strategy-specific, and how exceptions are governed and tested.

G. Trade rejection authority.
Rama's rough examples suggest rejecting a trade when SL/target cannot fit relative to S&R. This must not become a live rejection gate by implication. Explicit governance must decide whether S&R is informational, affects ranking, affects Trade/Reject, or participates in entry/risk calculations.

H. Data quality and missing levels.
Recording and alerting is a preliminary preference. Whether a signal proceeds, waits, or is rejected when S&R is unavailable, stale, inconsistent, or suspect remains open. Any pre-flight behavior must be defined so it does not accidentally become an unapproved hidden gate.

I. Shadow / retrospective evidence.
A log-only evidence phase is worth evaluating, but its duration, sample size, evidence measures, acceptance criteria, ownership, and approval gate are not decided.

J. Legacy claims.
Rama does not recall the specific old component or testing-VM behavior. Stage 1 evidence does not establish that the detector actually ran on a host or affected a live trade. Keep that historical question UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED unless valid evidence later establishes it.
