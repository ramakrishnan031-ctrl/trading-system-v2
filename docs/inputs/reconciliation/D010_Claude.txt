CLARIFICATION 1 — WHAT IS MEANT BY AN S&R ZONE?

Rama-authorised clarification, drafted by ChatGPT:

“My understanding is that Support and Resistance should be identified from historical price behaviour. A trader looks back at the chart to identify the important price points and price areas where the stock repeatedly reacted, reversed, consolidated, or changed direction.

I do not mean that every S&R reference must be represented only as one exact price. A price point can be a useful reference, but the historical reactions may occur across a surrounding price area. Therefore, the useful representation may need to show the reference price as well as the broader reaction area around it.

In the BANKBARODA example, the displayed reference around ₹277.94 is close to a wider historical reaction/congestion area around approximately ₹276–₹280. The line and the broader reaction area are related, but they should not automatically be treated as having identical boundaries.

The purpose is to understand the historically relevant price area that the current price may approach and react to. The exact method for identifying the point, defining the area's boundaries, or deciding whether to display one or both is not being specified here.”

Interpretation/limits:
- This answer indicates that both a reference price and a surrounding reaction area may be useful.
- It does not settle whether V2 must use points, zones or both. D-009 OQ9-25 remains OPEN.
- It does not define a zone-width formula, tolerance, pivot rule, touch count, candle rule, timeframe or algorithm.
- The BANKBARODA values are approximate visual observations from the supplied BSE daily chart, not verified market-data values or frozen test levels.

CLARIFICATION 2 — WHAT IS THE “NEXT EXPECTED PRICE RANGE” AFTER A BREAKOUT/BREAKDOWN?

Rama-authorised clarification, drafted by ChatGPT:

“The next expected price range is not the same thing as the S&R area that price has just broken. It refers to the next historically identified S&R reference or reaction area in the direction of the price movement.

For example, if the stock is approaching a resistance area and breaks above it, the trader looks further back in the historical chart to identify the next relevant resistance area above the breakout. Similarly, if the stock breaks below a support area, the trader looks for the next relevant support area below it.

So, the S&R area being tested or broken is one reference; the next historical S&R area in the direction of movement is a separate reference. It is a possible area for price to reach or react around, not a guaranteed destination or prediction.

The BANKBARODA example illustrates this distinction: the area around ₹277–₹280 is the resistance area being approached/tested in the example, while the lower historical area around ₹256–₹257 is a separate support reference. The example does not establish a general calculation rule or guarantee that price will travel from one area to the other.”

Interpretation/limits:
- “Next” means the next relevant historical S&R reference/area in the direction being considered, not the same broken/tested area.
- This is a clarification of terminology and intent only.
- It does not define how to rank competing areas, calculate “nearest”, select a timeframe, estimate probability, set a target, calculate R:R, or decide Trade/Reject.
- D-009 OQ9-13 and OQ9-14 remain OPEN. Target and R:R mechanics remain with their proper E topics.

CLARIFICATION 3 — WHAT DOES “COMMON” S&R MEAN?

Rama's direct clarification, based on his latest message:

“S&R points and S&R-related price areas are not strategy-specific. They are a common, instrument-level market reference. The historical support and resistance behaviour of a particular stock should be identified independently of which trading strategy has generated a signal for that stock.

The same underlying S&R references for that instrument can therefore be relevant to different strategies. The strategies may have different entry conditions or use the common S&R information differently, but the underlying S&R points/areas themselves should not be separately created merely because the signal came from a different strategy.

By ‘common’, I mean common across strategies for the same instrument. I do not mean that all stocks share the same S&R prices or that every instrument has one common set of price levels.”

Interpretation/limits:
- This records a global/common S&R concept at the instrument level, shared across strategies.
- It does not mean one universal price map across all symbols.
- It does not decide how strategy-specific entry, breakout, pullback, filtering or trade-decision rules consume S&R.
- D-009 OQ9-20 remains OPEN for the decision/use implications. No strategy-specific gate or decision authority is approved.
