REQUIRED INVESTIGATION METHOD
For every important flow or suspected issue:
- Trace from the true entry point through callers, callees, shared helpers, configuration, persistence, and downstream consumers.
- Inspect both sides of module boundaries. Do not infer behavior from function names or comments alone.
- Follow the actual values and units through calculations. Identify source, transformations, rounding, storage, and later reuse.
- Check all relevant call sites, not just the first occurrence.
- Compare duplicate or parallel implementations and determine whether they are equivalent.
- Inspect both success and failure paths, including timeout, retry, partial completion, restart, concurrency, and recovery behavior.
- Check whether validation is enforced at the correct layer and whether it can be bypassed.
- Check whether state transitions are atomic, idempotent, and recoverable.
- Distinguish a code defect from a documentation gap, operational uncertainty, design weakness, or unverified runtime claim.
- Look for interactions between individually reasonable modules that produce an unsafe combined outcome.
- Do not stop at symptoms. Establish root cause, trigger conditions, affected paths, and blast radius where evidence permits.
- Where evidence is insufficient, state exactly what is missing and what safe evidence would resolve it. Do not invent runtime facts.
